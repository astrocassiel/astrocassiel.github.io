PhD Research Highlights

Two-Color Thermography for Metal Additive Manufacturing

My doctoral research explores how two-color thermography can be used as a scalable sensing framework for metal additive manufacturing. Rather than relying on extensive instrumentation, this approach uses a single commercial camera to extract multiple process outputs of interest, including melt pool temperature, cooling rate, thermal gradients, and geometric features.

Wire Arc Additive Manufacturing & Thermal History

My work studies how thermal history evolves during WAAM and how it influences melt pool behavior, solidification conditions, and resulting properties. This includes process parameter studies, layer-resolved thermal analysis, and relationships between thermal signatures and deposited part geometry.

Thermography System Development & Calibration

I have developed experimental workflows for reliable two-color thermography using commercial imaging hardware, including calibration, exposure optimization, and strategies to reduce arc and plasma interference during welding.

Alloy 718 WAAM Process Monitoring

A major focus of my research is multi-layer Alloy 718 WAAM, where I analyze interlayer dwell time, thermal accumulation, cooling rate, and melt pool evolution to better understand scalable process monitoring and future feedback control strategies.
