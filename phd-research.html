PhD Research | Carnegie Mellon University, Department of Mechianical Engineering

Wire-Arc Additive Manufacturing, thermal monitoring, and control for advanced manufacturing.

Doctoral Thesis

0. Preface

My doctoral research centers on in-situ thermal monitoring for wire arc additive manufacturing (WAAM), with the goal of improving process understanding, defect detection, and closed-loop control in metal additive manufacturing.

1. Introduction to Wire Arc Additive Manufacturing

This chapter introduces WAAM as a large-scale metal additive manufacturing process, highlighting its advantages, process variables, and the importance of thermal history in determining melt pool behavior, microstructure, and mechanical performance.

2. Optimizing Camera Parameters for Two-Color Thermography

This work establishes the imaging conditions needed for reliable two-color thermography using a commercial color camera, including calibration strategy, exposure selection, and methods to reduce arc and plasma interference during welding.

3. Two-Color Thermography of L-59 Low-Carbon Steel

This chapter applies two-color thermography to gas metal arc welding of L-59 low-carbon steel, demonstrating how measured thermal fields can be used to extract cooling rates and correlate them with resulting hardness.

4. Two-Color Thermography of Cold-Metal Transfer Alloy 718

This work extends the thermography framework to multi-layer Alloy 718 WAAM builds, where thermal accumulation, interlayer dwell time, and melt pool evolution are analyzed to better understand their influence on solidification behavior and mechanical response.

5. Multi-Spectral Thermography for Aluminum Alloy Welding

This chapter explores multi-spectral imaging approaches for aluminum alloy welding, expanding beyond two-color measurements to improve temperature sensing in materials and process conditions where emissivity and optical behavior are more complex.
